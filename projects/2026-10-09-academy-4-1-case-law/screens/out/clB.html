States Employment Board v Francioso
Employment law
Employment
18 Sept 2026

Royal Court appeal concerning the Employment & Discrimination Tribunal's refusal to award costs to the States Employment Board. The Deputy Bailiff allowed the appeal on two grounds of law: (1) that Article 40A of the Procedure Order establishes a punitive costs regime to sanction and deter unreasonable conduct, not a compensatory regime; and (2) that the Tribunal erred in requiring demonstration of 'wasted costs' as a prerequisite to awarding costs under Article 40A.

ABC v Huntercombe (no.12) Limited
ABC / Huntercombe (no.12) Limited, Active Young People Limited, Dr Veerraju Banisetti, Dr Mark Tattersall
Employment law
Commercial
8 Sept 2026
|
Transfer Of Undertakings
|
Judge
Coulson Lj, Bean Lj, Henderson J
Outcome
The Court of Appeal dismissed the appeal, holding that an employer's vicarious liability to a third party for employee torts does not transfer to the transferee under TUPE 2006 Regulation 4(2)(a), as the Directive's purpose is to safeguard employee rights, not third-party claims.
ABC claimed damages for alleged abuse and restraint during her placement at Huntercombe Hospital in 2018/2019. After Huntercombe transferred to Active Young People Limited under TUPE in March 2021, ABC sought to hold the transferee vicariously liable for the acts of employees. The Court of Appeal dismissed the appeal, holding that vicarious liability to third parties does not transfer under TUPE Regulation 4(2)(a) because the Directive's purpose is to safeguard employee rights, not to extend liability to transferees for pre-transfer third-party claims.
Key Holdings
-
Vicarious liability of an employer to a third party does not constitute a right enforceable by the employee and therefore does not transfer under Regulation 4(2)(a)
-
The purpose of the Acquired Rights Directive is to safeguard employee rights on transfer, not to protect third-party claimants
-
Regulation 4(2)(a) liabilities must arise in connection with the contract of employment and be enforceable by the employee
-
The absence of provisions in TUPE Regulations requiring disclosure of third-party claims to the transferee indicates Parliament's deliberate policy to exclude such claims from transfer
-
Vicarious liability is a secondary, parasitic liability dependent on the employee's primary liability and provides no substantive protection to the employee
-
An employer's entitlement to a Lister indemnity from the employee means the employee bears ultimate liability regardless of whether the employer is vicariously liable
