Checking the same clause in every contract, one by one, all evening? Let Andri check them all at once, in one table.

Here, six supplier contracts for our client, Pennbridge Foods, are already in the matter's Knowledge Base.

Open Review from the sidebar. Each document becomes a row, and each question a column.

Click Add a source. Pick from this matter's Knowledge Base, your firm's, or case law. Open the folder, select all six, and use them.

Now the table has six rows, one for each contract. Each column will be one question, which Andri asks of every contract.

Click the empty column to write your first question. First, pick a format. It decides what kind of answer you get back: short text, yes or no, an exact extract, a date, a number, or a choice list.

This one is a yes or no question. Give the column a short name, Liability cap. Then type the question itself: does the contract cap the supplier's liability?

Click Done. The column is ready, but its cells stay empty. Nothing runs until you say so.

Add as many questions as you need. Here, a second column for when each contract ends, as a date. And a third for the notice period, as a number.

Three questions for six contracts makes eighteen cells, counted at the top.

Before running all eighteen, try one cell. Andri answers the first question for the first contract only. If the answer isn't what you meant, change the question now.

Happy with it? Click Start review. Every cell is queued, then Andri answers them, one by one, until all eighteen are filled in.

Each cell holds the answer, a short reason, and the clause and page it comes from.

Click the source to see the exact passage, with the document and the page.

Now put the table to work. Filter the Liability cap column to show only the No answers: the two contracts with no cap.

Need one more question later? Add another column. Only its six new cells run. The answers you already have stay as they are.

When you're done, export the whole table to Excel, with every answer and its reasoning.
