The other side's redline lands at midnight. A ready-made workflow can have it reviewed and marked up by morning. Here's how to start from one, and make it your own.

Open Explore, then Workflows, and click New Workflow.

This time, choose Start from a template.

Start blank, or from one of four examples: a contract redline response, a daily morning briefing, a matter status memo, and a full intake for new clients.

Open the contract redline response. All six steps are already in place.

It starts when an email arrives with a Word document attached. First, Andri checks that it really is a contract with tracked changes.

Then it reviews every change against the facts of your case, and rates the risk.

Next, it counter-edits the redline in place: new tracked changes and comments, inside the same Word document.

Last, it writes a short covering note and emails it to you, with the document attached. Nothing goes back to the other side until you've checked it.

Now the intake example. Say a prospective client, like Priya Nair, emails in after her dismissal.

Andri first checks the matter is one your firm handles. If it isn't, the client is politely referred elsewhere.

Then it checks the file is complete. If something is missing, it asks the client for it.

If everything is there, Andri triages the matter and builds a dossier for the lawyer.

Nothing is written for the client until the lawyer approves. Then Andri drafts the advice report and the next letter or email, and sends both to you to check.

To make it yours, change what doesn't fit. Aldridge Vine only takes on employment work, so say that in the first check, and save.

And send every approval to one person: Adam Frith.

Give it a name, and click Publish. It's now a template, ready to switch on in a matter.

Go to the matter whose email address receives your enquiries, open its Workflows, and click Activate.

The example already listens for the word intake in the subject line. Connect your Outlook or Gmail with sending turned on, then click Activate.

Start from an example, change what doesn't fit, and switch it on.
